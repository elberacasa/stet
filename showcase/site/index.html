stet: this path is governed by a decision the human has not made yet. api-shape — "Which shape should the list endpoint return?" claims: src/api/** Do not implement either option yet — whichever you pick has a 50% chance of being thrown away. Either work somewhere else, or block on the verdict with: stet await api-shape

That is a coding agent being stopped mid-write, because you had not decided yet.

Agents hit forks with no right answer — which type, which copy, how much abstraction — and they guess, because they are trained to finish. stet refuses the write, asks you once, and makes your verdict binding on every agent that touches the repo from then on.

npx stetmark # in any repo

Zero runtime dependencies. Nothing leaves your machine. No account, no key, no network.

the loop one command, any repo
1
An agent hits a fork it cannot derive and runs stet ask. It writes a small file, blocks on a file watch, and burns nothing while it waits.
2
You get a notification. Whenever you feel like it, you open a page showing the real thing — matched captures, live pages, audio, whatever the choice is made of.
3
You pick, blind, and say why. Committing reveals what A and B actually were — and that is the first moment you can write a rule worth obeying.
4
The rule lands in your canon and syncs into every agent instruction surface in the repo. The agent unblocks and continues with your answer.
5
Next week a different agent, on a different machine, obeys it without asking.
why it is cheaper, not slower
claimmeasured
A waiting agent burns nothing. stet await sleeps on a file watch rather than polling. 17.08s blocked on 0.09s of CPU, and the counter did not move while waiting.
Rules arrive when they apply, not all at once at session start. A 40-rule canon: 1046 always-resident tokens → 538 delivered just in time, 20 rules never sent.
The canon stays small. A hard budget holds rules back rather than truncating silently. 1 rule = 65 tokens. 40 rules = 971, measured with a real BPE tokenizer.
The gate costs nothing to run. It sits in a hook on every write. 50ms per invocation; 1.3ms to decide after 1500 files; 5.0ms against 1200 rules.
what it is not

Not an agent orchestrator. Not a dashboard. Not traffic-based A/B testing. Not a hosted service, an account, or telemetry. It is a gate, and anything can feed it.
